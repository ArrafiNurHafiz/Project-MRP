User Guide

Complete step-by-step guide to using Labamu Manufacturing (MRP)

This guide walks you through the manufacturing workflow, from master data to sales, production, and reporting. Menu labels below match the sidebar; your role may hide some items.

What you need to know:

  • MRP is available once your business is activated for the Manufacturing app on Labamu.
  • Core master data includes Products, Materials, Bill of Materials (BOM), and Routing. Vendors and Customers live under Resources.
  • Typical end-to-end flow:
    Master data (materials, routing, BOM, products) → Sales (RFQ → Quotes → Orders) → Work Order (allocation & production) → Invoices & completion
  • Main sidebar areas:
    • Dashboard — overview
    • Products — Products Catalog, Custom Products Request
    • Sales — Request For Quotes, Quotes, Orders, Invoices, Customers
    • Resources — Materials, Vendors, Material Requests, Purchase Orders
    • Manufacturing — Work Order, Bill of Materials, Routing
    • Analytics — Financial Report, Inventory Report, Sales Funnel Report, Work Order Monitoring, Procurement & AP Report
    • Administration — User Management, FX Management, Company Settings
    • User Guide — this page
  • Load Materials, Routing, BOM, and Products before using RFQs, Quotes, and Orders that depend on them.
  • To load materials and products in bulk instead of one at a time, use Bulk Upload from Materials or Products Catalog — see Bulk Upload Materials in Step 2 and Bulk Upload Products in Step 5.

How to Create Materials:

  1. Go to Resources → Materials in the sidebar.
  2. Click New Material.
  3. Fill in the details as shown below:
    • SKU — Auto-generated if left empty.
    • Material Name — (Mandatory) Enter the material name (e.g., Steel Plate 2mm).
    • Category — (Mandatory) Select or create a material category (e.g., Metal, Plastic).
    • ABC Classification — (Mandatory) A, B, or C by value or importance.
    • Material Type — (Mandatory) Raw Material, Semi-Finished Material, or Finished Material.
    • Unit of Measure — (Mandatory) Select or create the unit (e.g., pcs, kg).
    • Stock Risk — (Optional) Running-low threshold to highlight materials before they run out.
    • Description — (Optional) Short note about the material.
    • Material Images — (Optional) Up to one image on create; supported formats include JPEG and PNG.
  4. Click Save at the top right.

Tips:

  • SKU will be automatically generated if left blank.
  • You can use Bulk Upload to import multiple materials from a spreadsheet at once — see Bulk Upload Materials below in this step.
  • Materials must be active to be used in BOMs and work orders.
  • Maintain consistent naming conventions for easier tracking and reporting.

Bulk Upload Materials:

Import many materials at once from a spreadsheet instead of creating them one by one. Your columns are mapped and normalized automatically, and you review every row before anything is saved.

Where to find it:

  • Go to Resources → Materials and click Bulk Upload at the top right.
  • It opens a Bulk Upload list that keeps every material upload you have made, so you can resume or review them later.
  • You need create access for Materials to start a new upload.

The flow has three steps: Upload → Mapping → Review. You can leave the page between steps and pick the upload up again from the Bulk Upload list.

Upload step — choose your file

  1. Click New Upload in the Bulk Upload list.
  2. Click Download Template to get the ready-made material spreadsheet. Using the template is optional, but it makes mapping and normalization far more accurate.
  3. Drag and drop your file or click to browse:
    • Max 1 file, 25MB.
    • Allowed formats: .xlsx, .xls, .csv.
  4. Click Analyze File. Reading the file usually takes a few seconds.
    Note: If you leave while the file is being analyzed, the analysis is aborted and you will need to upload again.

How your columns are matched:

  • When you click Analyze File, AI reads your header row and matches each column to the app's material fields, so your spreadsheet does not have to use our column names or our column order.
  • What it matched is shown in the next step under AI Recommendation. It is only a recommendation — you can change or clear any row before you continue.
  • If AI is unavailable or fails, the app checks whether your file is the downloaded material template. If it is, the template's own headers are used for the mapping instead and you carry on as normal.
  • A file counts as the template only when it is the .xlsx template with its header row untouched — renaming, reordering, or removing template columns (or saving it as .csv) breaks the match.
  • If AI fails and the file is not the template, the analysis cannot continue and you will be asked to upload again — one more reason to start from the template.

Mapping step — check the column mapping

  1. Your columns are matched to the app's material fields automatically. The table shows four columns:
    • Material Field — the field in the app, with a Required tag where a value is mandatory.
    • Source Column — the column from your file that feeds it. Change it or clear it to "- Not mapped -" at any time.
    • Example Value — a real value from your file, so you can confirm the match is right.
    • AI Recommendation — which column was matched, or "No match found".
  2. Map every Required field.
  3. Click Normalize and Review. Your data is cleaned up and validated to prepare it for review.
  4. Normalizing a large file takes a while. You can leave the page and you will get an email when it is ready, or click Skip AI Normalization to stop early — rows that were not normalized yet will simply need your attention in the Review step.

What normalization does:

  • Once you confirm the mapping, AI goes through your rows and rewrites the values into the form each material field expects — lining category names up with the categories you already use, reading your own wording into Classification (A, B, C), Type (Raw, Semi Finished, Finished) and Active or Inactive status, matching units of measurement to the ones you have, and tidying up number formatting.
  • Values it cannot interpret are left as they are and flagged as needing attention, so nothing is quietly guessed for you.
  • The Review step shows how far it got, as "X of Y rows normalized by AI".
  • Normalization never imports anything on its own — every row still goes through the Review step, where you can edit it.

Review step — check before importing

The review screen shows every material row from your file as an editable grid.

  • Turn on "Show only need attention data" to see just the rows with missing or invalid required values.
  • Use the search box to find rows by SKU, name, or category, and sort or page through the grid as usual.
  • Click New Row to add a material that was missing from your file.
  • Tick the rows you do not want and click Delete to drop them from this upload.
  • Click Save as Draft to stop and come back later. The upload keeps the Review status in the list.
    Note: Edits you have not saved are lost if you refresh or leave the page without saving.
  • Click Import Data when you are done.
  • Importing runs in the background — you can leave the page and you will get an email when it is finished.

Which material fields are required:

  • Material Name
  • Category
  • ABC Classification
  • Material Type
  • Unit of Measurement

Tracking your material uploads:

The Bulk Upload list shows File Name, Upload ID, Created At, Created By, Total Data, and Status. Filter by status or search by file name or upload ID to find an older upload.

  • Mapping — waiting for you to confirm the column mapping.
  • Normalizing Data — your data is being cleaned up and validated.
  • Review — ready for you to review the rows, including uploads you saved as a draft.
  • Processing — the import is running.
  • Completed — the materials are in your material list.
  • Cancelled — you stopped the upload; it stays here as a record.

Clicking a row that is still in progress takes you back to where you left off. Rows that are Processing, Completed, or Cancelled open a detail view instead, showing the upload ID, file name, who created it and when, and how much data was in the file, kept after review, imported, and invalid.

Tips:

  • Start from the downloaded material template whenever you can — the mapping step then usually needs no changes at all.
  • Leave SKU empty and it will be generated for you, the same as when you create a material by hand.
  • Once an upload is Completed, open its detail and click Download Invalid Data to get the rows that did not make it, fix them, and upload that file again.
  • Importing only the ready rows is a good way to get moving on a large file — the rows that need attention can be corrected and uploaded separately.
  • Keep category and unit-of-measurement names spelled consistently with what you already use, so you do not end up with near-duplicates.

How to Create Batches:

Each material can have multiple batches to track stock quantity, expiry, and vendor details. This helps you manage materials more accurately.

  1. Open the material you want to manage (for example, Table).
  2. Go to the Stock Batches tab.
  3. Click + New Batch.
  4. Fill in the following details:
    • Initial Quantity — (Mandatory) Enter the total quantity received or produced (e.g., 100).
    • Current Quantity — (Mandatory) Enter the remaining stock for this batch (e.g., 100).
      Note: Current quantity cannot exceed the initial quantity.
    • Cost per Unit — (Optional) Input the cost of the material per unit. (e.g., 100.000)
    • Purchase Date — (Mandatory) Select the date when the material was received or purchased.
    • Expiry Date — (Optional) Add an expiry or best-before date if applicable.
    • Status — (Mandatory) Choose one of the following:
      • Requested - Batch requested but not yet received.
      • Expected - Batch is expected to arrive.
      • Delayed - Delivery delayed.
      • Received - Material has been received and available in stock.
    • Storage Location — (Optional) Enter where the batch is stored (e.g., Warehouse A, Rack 3B).
    • Vendor — (Optional) Select the vendor if applicable.
  5. After filling all fields, click Save Batch.
  6. To add another batch, click + New Batch again.

Notes:

  • The system automatically generates a Batch ID — no need to fill it manually.
  • You can filter batches by Status, Vendor, or Expiry using the search options below the batch list.
  • Use Show Empty Batches to display all inactive or depleted batches.
  • Always ensure purchase and expiry dates are valid to avoid errors.

Material Requests (Resources):

Use Resources → Material Requests to prepare or track material needs separately from the materials list. Exact steps depend on your process; open a request from the list to view details, activity, and related actions your permissions allow.

How to Create Routing:

  1. Go to Manufacturing → Routing in the sidebar.
  2. Click Add Routing on the top right.
  3. In the dialog, fill in:
    • Routing Name — (Mandatory) Name of this routing definition (e.g., Cutting, Assembly).
    • Routing Description — (Optional) Notes about the operation.
  4. Save to add it to the routing list. Create more routings as needed.

Notes:

  • You can create multiple routing stages to define your full production workflow.
  • Keep stage names clear and consistent (e.g., Cutting → Welding → Painting → Quality Check).
  • Routings are referenced from BOMs (per production step) and drive work order routing stages and progress.

How to Create a BOM:

  1. Go to Manufacturing → Bill of Materials.
  2. On the list, click New Mapping to open the Create Bill of Materials screen.
  3. Under Bill of Materials Information:
    • BOM Name — (Mandatory)
    • Description — (Optional)
  4. Under Materials, click Add Material. Pick a material and quantity per unit of output. Repeat for all components. At least one material is required to save.
  5. Under Routing, click Add Routing. In the dialog:
    • Routing — (Mandatory) Choose a routing definition created in Step 3.
    • Operation — (Mandatory) Name for this step (e.g., Cut steel).
    • Step Number — Filled automatically in sequence.
    • Hours — (Mandatory) Estimated hours for this step.
    Add every production step in order. At least one routing row is required to save.
  6. Review costs (material roll-up plus labor, packing, and other costs as shown on the form) so totals match how you plan to price and track COGS.
  7. Click Save at the top right.

Notes:

  • BOMs can be active or inactive; inactive BOMs are unlinked from products as applicable.
  • Only active BOMs should be used when linking products for sales.

How to Create a Product:

  1. Go to Products in the sidebar.
  2. Click New Product at the top right.
  3. Fill in the Basic Information section:
    • Product Name – (Mandatory) Enter the product name (e.g., Stainless Steel Table).
    • SKU – (Optional) Automatically generated if left empty (e.g., TBL-002).
    • Category – (Mandatory) Select or create a product category (e.g., Furniture, Electronics).
    • Status – (Mandatory) Choose Active if the product should be available for use (e.g., Active).
    • Description – (Optional) Add additional details about the product.
  4. (Optional) Upload Product Images:
    • Click Select Images or drag and drop image files.
    • The first uploaded image will be set as the primary display image.
  5. In the Pricing & Logistics section:
    • Bill of Materials – (Optional) Select the BOM that defines how this product is made.
    • Base Price – (Mandatory) Input the cost per unit (e.g., IDR 100.000).
    • Selling Price – (Mandatory) Enter the final price that will appear in sales modules (e.g., IDR 200.000).
    • Lead Time – (Mandatory) Define the estimated production time (e.g., 2 weeks).
  6. Click Save at the top right corner to create the product.

Notes:

  • Products can be linked directly to a BOM and Routing to define how they're produced.
  • Keep product names clear and consistent with BOM naming conventions.
  • You can edit or deactivate products anytime from the product list.
  • Uploading product images helps for catalog or sales purposes.
  • To create many products at once, use Bulk Upload from the Products Catalog — see Bulk Upload Products below in this step.
  • Products without a BOM cannot be used when creating RFQs, Quotes, or Orders.
  • Inactive products cannot be used when creating RFQs, Quotes, or Orders.

How to Create an RFQ:

  1. Go to Sales → Request For Quotes.
  2. Click New RFQ to open the creation form.
  3. Fill in the Customer Information section:
    • Select Customer – (Optional) Choose an existing customer or left it blank if u want create new customer
    • Customer Name – (Mandatory) Fill in relevant customer name (e.g., XYZ Corp.).
    • Customer Email – (Optional) Fill in relevant customer email (e.g., admin@xyz.com).
    • Customer Phone – (Optional) Fill in relevant customer phone (e.g., +6287384748392).
    • Customer Address – (Mandatory) Fill in relevant customer address (e.g., Street Wall No.24).
    • Add Multiple PIC (Max 5 PIC). Each PIC includes the following fields:
      • PIC Primary – Flag to mark as the primary PIC (only one PIC should be primary).
      • PIC Name – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's name (e.g., John Doe).
      • PIC Email – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's email (e.g., johndoe@mail.com).
      • PIC Phone – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's phone number (e.g., +6287384748392).
      • PIC Role – (Mandatory) Select Viewer or Approver. This determines actions allowed on the Microsite.
  4. Under Products, click Add Product:
    • Select products.
    • In the Add Product to Quote popup:
      • Product – (Mandatory) Select or search for the product.
      • Quantity – (Mandatory) Input the number of units (e.g., 2).
      • Attachments – (Optional) Upload up to 3 files (max 5 MB each).
  5. (Optional) Upload relevant Attachments such as product specifications or technical drawings.
  6. Add Additional Information:
    • Customer Notes - (Optional)
    • Internal Notes – (Optional) Notes for internal team reference only.
  7. Once completed, choose one of the following actions:
    • Save Draft – To continue editing later.
    • Submit – To proceed for approval.

Custom Products in an RFQ:

  • If an RFQ contains a product of type Custom, the system automatically generates a Custom Products Request (CPR) when the RFQ is submitted.
  • The internal team must fill and complete the CPR before the RFQ can proceed to Quotation (see Step 7).

Approval Flow:

  • Submitted RFQs will follow the Approval Rules defined in the RFQ Settings.
  • If no approval rule exists, the RFQ will be auto-approved and automatically move to the Quotation stage.
  • If approval is required:
    • The RFQ will be routed to the designated approver.
    • If approved, it proceeds to Quotation creation.
    • If rejected, the RFQ cannot continue to the next stage.

How it is triggered:

  1. When an RFQ is submitted and contains at least one product with type Custom, the system automatically generates a CPR linked to that RFQ.
  2. Go to Products → Custom Products Request to find the generated CPR.
  3. Click the CPR row to open its detail page.

How to Fill a CPR (Internal Team):

  1. Open the CPR from the list.
  2. Click Fill CPR to open the fill form.
  3. Complete the product specification fields:
    • Product Name — (Mandatory) Name of the custom product.
    • Category — (Mandatory) Select or create a product category.
    • Unit of Measure — (Mandatory) Select the unit.
    • Base Price / Selling Price — (Mandatory) Set cost and selling price.
    • Lead Time — (Mandatory) Estimated production time.
    • Materials — Add at least one material with quantity.
    • Routing Stages — Add at least one routing step with hours.
    • Engineering Notes — (Optional) Internal technical notes.
    • Product Image — (Optional) Upload a reference image.
  4. Choose one of the following actions:
    • Save Draft — Save for further editing.
    • Submit — Send for approval.

CPR Status Flow:

  • New — Auto-generated from the RFQ; not yet filled.
  • Draft — Being filled, not yet submitted.
  • Submitted — Awaiting internal approval.
  • Need Revision — Approver requested changes before approval.
  • Completed — Approved; the custom product is ready and the RFQ can proceed to Quotation.
  • Rejected — Request declined and cannot proceed.
  • Pending Re-Evaluation — Awaiting re-evaluation after a revision request.

Approval Flow:

  • Approval rules are configured in Custom Products Request → Settings.
  • If no approval rule exists, submitting a CPR automatically completes it.
  • Once Completed, the RFQ can proceed to the Quotation stage.

How to Create a Quote:

  1. Navigate to Sales → Quotes in the sidebar.
  2. Click Create Quote on the top-right corner.
  3. Fill Quote Details:
    • Currency – Select the transaction currency (e.g., IDR – Indonesian Rupiah).
    • Down Payment Percentage – Define the required down payment (optional, default 0%).
  4. Fill Customer Information:
    • Select Customer – (Optional) Choose an existing customer or left it blank if u want create new customer.
    • Customer Name – (Mandatory) Fill in relevant customer name (e.g., XYZ Corp.).
    • Customer Email – (Optional) Fill in relevant customer email (e.g., admin@xyz.com).
    • Customer Phone – (Optional) Fill in relevant customer phone (e.g., +6287384748392).
    • Customer Address – (Mandatory) Fill in relevant customer address (e.g., Street Wall No.24).
    • Add Multiple PIC (Max 5 PIC). Each PIC includes the following fields:
      • PIC Primary – Flag to mark as the primary PIC (only one PIC should be primary).
      • PIC Name – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's name (e.g., John Doe).
      • PIC Email – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's email (e.g., johndoe@mail.com).
      • PIC Phone – (Mandatory) Enter the PIC's phone number (e.g., +6287384748392).
      • PIC Role – (Mandatory) Select Viewer or Approver. This determines actions allowed on the Microsite.
  5. Add Products to the Quote:
    • Click + Add Product.
    • In the Add Product to Quote popup:
      • Product – Select or search for the product.
      • Quantity – (Mandatory) Input the number of units (e.g., 2).
      • Unit Price – (Mandatory) Enter the price per unit (e.g., IDR 200.000).
      • Discount – (Optional) Add a discount if applicable (e.g., 0%).
      • Total Price – Automatically calculated based on quantity and discount (e.g., IDR 200.000).
      • Notes – (Optional) Add internal or customer notes.
      • Attachments – (Mandatory) Upload up to 3 files (max 5 MB each), such as product specs documents.
  6. Total Amount:
    After products are added, the system automatically calculates totals. Complete the following fields:
    • Subtotal – Total before taxes or fees (e.g., IDR 200.000).
    • Tax Rate – (Optional) Input tax rate (e.g., 10%).
    • Tax Total – Total updates automatically (e.g., IDR 20.000).
    • Shipping Fee – (Optional) Optional shipping cost (e.g., IDR 10.000).
    • Other Fee – (Optional) Add additional charges if any (e.g., IDR 20.000).
    • Total – Final amount after tax and all fees (e.g., IDR 250.000).
  7. Add Attachments (Optional):
    Upload general files related to the quotation, such as proposals, photos, or supporting documents. You can upload up to 3 files (max 5 MB each).
  8. Fill Terms & Conditions:
    Define agreement details before submission:
    • Payment Terms – (Mandatory) Select available payment options (e.g., NET 30 – payment due in 30 days after invoice, NET 60, or Cash Before Delivery).
    • Incoterms – (Optional) Select delivery terms if applicable (e.g., FOB – Free on Board, CIF – Cost, Insurance, and Freight, or EXW – Ex Works).
    • Risk Level – Automatically filled with the project or transaction risk level based on the selected Incoterms (e.g., Medium Risk for international shipment using CIF terms).
    • Estimated Delivery – (Optional) Input expected delivery time (e.g., 2–3 weeks after order confirmation).
    • Governing Law – (Optional) Define applicable law or jurisdiction (e.g., Indonesian Law or Singapore Commercial Law).
    • Shipping Method – (Optional) Choose the transportation method (e.g., Air Freight, Sea Cargo, or Local Courier).
    • Dispute Resolution Method – (Optional) Define how disputes will be resolved (e.g., Arbitration, Mediation, or Court Settlement).
    • Include Clauses – (Optional):
      • Include Force Majeure Clause
      • Include Late Payment Penalties
      • Include Performance Guarantees
  9. Final Actions:
    After filling all required details:
    • Save Draft – Save for later editing.
    • Submit – Send the quotation for approval.

Notes:

If you want to use a currency other than your base currency, please ensure your FX Management module is updated. You must input the latest FX exchange rate to ensure accurate conversion during quotation.

Quote Status Flow:

  • Draft – Quotation is being prepared and not yet submitted.
  • Submitted – Waiting for internal approval (based on approval rules).
  • Issued – Approved internally and sent to the customer.
  • Approved – Customer has accepted the quotation.
  • Rejected – Rejected by internal approver or customer.
  • Need Revision – Customer requested updates or corrections before approval.

Approval Flow:

  • Internal approval follows the rules defined in Quote Settings → Approval Rules.
  • Once internally approved, the quote changes to Issued and awaits customer approval.
  • The customer must approve before the quote can be converted into an Order.
  • If no approval rule exists, the system automatically moves from Submitted → Issued.

After Approval:

  • Once Approved by the customer:
    • The system automatically converts the quote into an Order and Work Order.
    • All pricing, product, and payment details are carried forward.
    • If Downpayment is filled, system will automatically create Downpayment Invoice.
  • Rejected quotes cannot proceed to the next stage.

How to View Orders:

  1. Go to Sales → Orders.
  2. Select the desired Order from the list to open the Order Details page.

How to Edit an Order:

  1. On the Order Details page, click Edit in the top-right corner.
  2. You can update the following fields:
    • Planned Start Date – Input the target start date for the order (e.g., 15 Oct 2025).
    • Planned End Date – Input the estimated completion date for the order (e.g., 25 Oct 2025).
    • Actual Start Date – Record the actual date when the order process begins (e.g., 16 Oct 2025).
    • Actual End Date – Record the actual date when the order process is completed (e.g., 24 Oct 2025).
    • Priority – Define the priority level of the order (e.g., Low, Medium, High, or Urgent).
    • Remarks – Add any additional notes or important information related to the order (e.g., "Expedite delivery due to customer request").
  3. You can also add Attachments related to the order (e.g., additional files, supporting documents).
  4. Click Save to confirm changes.

  2. The system will open the Shipment Details page.

Order Status Flow:

  • Not Started – Order has been created but not yet processed.
  • Waiting for Approval – Pending internal approval based on approval rules.
  • Confirmed – Order has been approved and is ready for manufacturing.
  • In Progress – Work Order related to this Order is ongoing.

Approval Flow:

  • Once approved, the Order status changes from Waiting for Approval → Confirmed.

How to Allocate Materials:

Before production starts, required materials must be covered for the work order. The work order shows a materials table (required vs received quantities). Allocation behavior depends on your company's allocation mode:

  • Automatic — When the work order is Ready to Process and not yet allocated, use Auto Allocate to reserve stock from inventory in one step (if stock is sufficient).
  • Request-based — Use Request Material and related flows so procurement can fulfill lines before they are received into stock.

General steps:

  1. Open the work order from the Work Order list.
  2. Ensure the work order reaches Ready to Process when required (including any steps from the related order).
  3. Use Auto Allocate or Request Material as applicable to your mode until required materials are satisfied.
  4. If automatic allocation fails, resolve stock shortages or requests, then try again.

Notes:

  • Routing progress is tied to successful material coverage.
  • With request-based mode, use View Request History when available to audit material requests for that work order.
  • Material requests are not limited to the Bill of Materials (BOM):
    • Exceeding BOM quantity — request more than the BOM-specified amount by selecting a reason: Material Wastage, Quality Testing, Safety Buffer, Rework, Buyer Change Request, Machine Setup, or Other.
    • Outside the BOM (Non-BOM) — request a material not listed in the BOM by selecting a reason: R&D / Trial Run, Design Change, Material Replacement, Extra Finishing, Production Consumable, Packaging Change, or Other.

    In both cases, add explanatory notes describing why the request is needed.

How to Manage Routing Stages:

After successful material allocation, you can manage and track progress for each Routing Stage.

To update routing progress:

  1. On the work order detail page, find the Routing Stages table.
  2. For each stage, open the management action (e.g., Manage).
  3. Update quantities across Yet to Start, In Progress, and completed buckets as shown in the dialog.
  4. Save progress. Totals and completion update from your entries.

Notes:

  • Routing stages can only be managed after successful material allocation.
  • Updating progress in any routing stage will automatically change the status to In Progress.
  • Use Start Process on the work order when you need to begin production explicitly.

How to Complete Work Order:

Once all routing stages are completed, mark the production as finished.

To complete:

  1. Verify that all routing stages are 100% completed.
  2. Click Complete Work Order.
  3. Confirm the completion.
  4. The Work Order status will change to Completed, and finished goods will be updated in inventory.

Work Order Status Flow:

  • Not Started – Work Order created but not yet processed.
  • Ready to Process – Materials allocated and ready for production.
  • In Progress – Routing stages are ongoing.
  • Completed – All stages are finished; goods updated in inventory.
  • Canceled – Work Order terminated or voided before completion.

How to Outsource a Routing Stage:

Each routing stage can be executed internally or delegated to an external vendor. The execution mode controls how each stage is handled:

  • Internal / In-House — Work done entirely by your own team.
  • Outsource — All units delegated to an external vendor.
  • Hybrid — Units split between your internal team and one or more external vendors.

To assign a vendor to an outsourced stage:

  1. Open the Work Order detail page and click Assign Vendor on the routing stage with mode Outsource or Hybrid.
  2. In the Add Assigned Vendor panel, fill in:
    • Vendor Name — (Required) Select an external vendor, or choose Internal for an internal worker.
    • Included Steps — (Required) Check one or more routing steps to include in this assignment. Multiple steps can be selected in a single assignment, but they must form a sequential (contiguous) range — you cannot skip a step in between.
    • Assigned Output — (Required) Number of units for this vendor; cannot exceed the available quantity of the selected steps.
    • Purchase Order — (Optional, shown once a non-Internal vendor is selected) Link an existing PO in Draft or Need Revision status for that vendor. If left empty, you can add a PO later after the assignment is saved.
  3. Click Save. The vendor appears in the stage's assignment list with their assigned and received quantities.

Notes:

  • Multiple vendors can be assigned to the same stage (Hybrid mode).
  • Once saved, the vendor on an assignment cannot be changed — only the assigned quantity and linked PO can be edited while the assignment is Not Started.
  • When a linked PO is approved (Issued), the PO number becomes visible in the outsource detail for that stage.

How to Link a Purchase Order Afterward:

If an assignment has a vendor but no linked PO yet, use the Add Purchase Order button on that assignment to either:

  • Select Existing PO — Choose an existing Draft or Need Revision PO for that vendor, then click Assign PO.
  • Create New PO — Opens the New Purchase Order page pre-filled with the vendor and assignment details.

How to Release to Vendor:

Before a vendor's delivered units can be received on the linked Purchase Order, the assigned quantity must first be released to that vendor from the Work Order.

  1. Open the Work Order and find the vendor assignment in the routing stage.
  2. Click the Release to Vendor icon (available once there is a quantity ready to release).
  3. Fill in:
    • Release Qty — Units to release; cannot exceed the available quantity for that assignment.
    • Notes — (Optional)
    • Upload Proof Document — (Required) At least one file with a description.
  4. Click Submit to confirm the release.

The assignment shows Ready to Release: {qty} when units are waiting to be released, or Waiting Receipt: {qty} once everything has been released but not yet received.

How Outsource Receipts Work:

Receipts for outsourced work are recorded from the Purchase Order → Receipt tab, not from the Work Order — see Manage Purchase Orders below. A unit can only be received once it has been released to the vendor from the Work Order.

How to View Release and Receipt Logs:

On the vendor assignment, click the Receipt History icon to open the Assignment Log panel. It has two tabs:

  • Released to Vendor — Date & Time, Release ID, Release Qty, Notes, Released by, and Document for each release.
  • Receipt — Date & Time, Receipt ID, Receipt Qty, Notes, Received by, and Document for each receipt.

Outsource Assignment Status:

  • Not Started — Assigned but no work begun.
  • In Progress — Vendor has started.
  • Partially Received — Some units received, awaiting the remainder.
  • Completed — All assigned units received.
  • Cancelled — Assignment was cancelled.

How to Create a Purchase Order:

  1. Go to Resources → Purchase Orders in the sidebar.
  2. Click New Purchase Order.
  3. Fill in the header:

    • Recipient — (Required) Your company's receiving entity.

PO Line Types:

Each line type determines what happens when the PO is approved and when receipts are recorded:

Manual

Purely informational. Enter the product name, SKU, quantity, and unit price manually. No side effects — this line is for documentation or cost-tracking only.

Material

Links to an existing material. When the PO is approved, the system automatically creates a stock batch for that material. Each time a receipt is recorded on this line, the batch's received quantity increases.

Work Order

Links to an outsourced Work Order routing stage. The PO number appears in the Work Order's outsource detail for the linked vendor assignment. When a receipt is recorded for this line, the outsource assignment's received quantity updates automatically.

PO Details Tab:

The PO Details tab is the default view when you open a PO. It shows a read-only summary of the entire purchase order:

  • Vendor Information — Name, phone, email, and address of the supplying vendor.
  • Recipient Information — Name, phone, email, and address of your company's receiving entity.
  • Purchase Order Lines — Table listing all ordered items with columns: Type (Manual, Material, or Work Order), Item Name, Description, Work Order Reference, Quantity, Unit Price, and Subtotal.

Receipt Tab:

The Receipt tab is available once the PO status is Issued. It has two sections: a receipt input table at the top for recording new deliveries, and a Receipt & Release Logs card at the bottom.

For Work Order lines, the assigned quantity must first be released to vendor from the Work Order (see Manage Work Order above) before it can be received here. If you try to receive more than what has been released, the line shows the error "Items must be released to vendor before receipt". Once some quantity is releasable, the field shows a "Ready to receive: {qty}" hint.

Receipt input table columns:

  • Received Qty — Quantity already received in prior receipts.
  • Remaining Qty — Outstanding quantity still to be received.

To record a receipt:

  1. Open the PO detail page and go to the Receipt tab.
  2. Enter the received quantity in the Receive Now column for each line.
  3. (Optional) Add notes and attach delivery documents.

    • Material lines — the linked stock batch received quantity increases.
    • Work Order lines — the outsource assignment received quantity updates.

Below the input table, the Receipt and Release Logs card has two tabs:

  • Receipt Logs — Receipt Date & Time, Receipt No, Item Name, SKU/Code, WO Reference, Assignment ID, Received Qty, Received by, Notes, and Proof Document for every past receipt.

3-Ways Match Tab:

The 3-Ways Match tab compares the ordered quantity on each PO line against the invoiced quantity and the received quantity, helping you identify discrepancies before payment.

  • Each row shows the item, PO Qty, Invoiced Qty, and Received Qty.
  • A green check means the quantity matches the PO; a red X means it does not yet match.
  • Hover over the icon to see the tooltip “Matched with purchase order qty” or “Still not matched with purchase order qty”.
  • Use this tab to confirm all three quantities align before closing out a PO or approving final payment.

Logs Tab:

The Logs tab provides a full audit trail for the PO. It contains two sections:

  • Approval Logs — Shown when an approval request exists for this PO. Displays who requested approval, when it was requested, and a table of approvers with their approval status and any comments.
  • Activity Logs — A paginated event history table with columns: Name (person who performed the action), Email, Activity (action type and description, e.g., PO Created, PO Updated, PO Cancelled), and Timestamp. Use this to trace every change made to the PO and by whom.

How to Revise a PO (PO Revision):

When a PO is in Issued status and changes are needed (e.g., quantity adjustments or updated fees), you can create a revised version without cancelling the existing PO.

  1. Open the PO detail page. At the bottom of the page, click PO Revise (visible only when the PO status is Issued and you have the revision permission).
  2. The Edit PO page opens in Revision Mode. Fields that can be changed in this mode:
    • Recipient — name, phone, email, address.
    • Notes & Terms
    • Tax Rate
    • Fees — add or edit fee amounts.
    • Line Quantity — for Material and Manual lines (Work Order lines cannot be edited).
  3. Fields that are locked in revision mode: Vendor, PO Date, Expected Delivery Date, Currency, line type, material/work order selection, and adding new lines.
  4. Click Submit. A confirmation modal appears — enter the Reason for Revision (required) and confirm.
  5. The system creates a new PO version (e.g., V1 → V2). You are redirected to the new version's detail page.

Viewing PO Versions:

  • On the PO detail page, a version badge (e.g., Version 2) appears next to the PO number. Click the chevron icon beside it to open the version selector dropdown.
  • Each entry in the dropdown shows the version label (V1, V2, …) and its creation date.
  • Click any version to navigate to that version's detail page.
  • When viewing a non-latest version, an info banner appears: “You are viewing Version X (view only).” The tabs (Receipts, Invoice & Payments, 3-Ways Match, Documents) are hidden; only the read-only detail section is shown.
